# Glossary

A list of terms used in the QES course.

```{glossary}
:sorted:

Abstraction
    The removal of water from groundwater or surface water, e.g. by pumping from wells.

Aquiclude
    Sometimes referred to as an *aquifuge*;
    a rock with very low {term}`hydraulic conductivity` that acts as an impermeable barrier to groundwater flow.

Aquifer
    A body of permeable rock or sediment that can store and transmit significant quantities of water.

Aquitard
    A rock with low values of {term}`hydraulic conductivity`, but that still allows some flow of groundwater through it.

Artesian Well
    A well that flows without pumping
    because the hydraulic head in the {term}`confined aquifer<Confined Aquifer>` that feeds it is greater than that at ground level.

Average Linear Velocity
Seepage Velocity
    The average speed at which water moves through the pores of a rock, $v = q/n_e$,
    where $q$ is the {term}`specific discharge` and $n_e$ is the effective {term}`porosity`.
    It is always greater than the {term}`specific discharge`.

Cone of Depression
    The cone-shaped {term}`drawdown` of the {term}`water table` or {term}`potentiometric surface` around a pumping well.

Confined Aquifer
    An {term}`aquifer` that is sealed by impermeable rock above and below.

Confining Layer
    A layer of low {term}`permeability` (an {term}`aquitard` or {term}`aquiclude`) that bounds a {term}`confined aquifer`.

Darcy's Law
    The law that the flow rate of water through a porous medium is proportional to the {term}`hydraulic gradient` and the cross-sectional area,
    $Q = -KA\dv{h}{l}$, where $K$ is the {term}`hydraulic conductivity`.

DIC
    **D**issolved **I**norganic **C**arbon - the sum of $CO_2$, $H_2CO_3$, $HCO_3^-$ and $CO_3^{2-}$ in water. 

Discharge
    The volume of water flowing past a particular point per unit time (e.g. in a river).
    In groundwater systems, discharge also refers to water leaving the groundwater system,
    e.g. into a river, lake, the ocean, or at a spring.

Drawdown
    The lowering of the {term}`water table` or {term}`potentiometric surface` caused by pumping.

Elevation Head
    The height in m above the reference datum, which is usually mean sea level in groundwater systems.

Equipotential Line
    A line joining points of equal {term}`hydraulic head`.
    In an isotropic {term}`aquifer`, groundwater flow lines cross equipotential lines at right angles.

Eutrophication
    The process by which a body of water becomes enriched in dissolved nutrients (such as phosphates) that stimulate the growth of aquatic plant life, usually resulting in the depletion of dissolved oxygen.

Evapotranspiration
    The combined loss of water to the atmosphere by evaporation from surfaces and transpiration from plants.

Groundwater Depletion
    The long-term decline in the amount of groundwater stored in an {term}`aquifer`, because {term}`abstraction` exceeds {term}`recharge`.

Hydraulic Conductivity
    A combination of the physical properties of a fluid and the {term}`permeability` of the medium it is flowing through which describes how quickly the fluid can move through it, termed $K$. This is a key property of hydrogeologic systems which is highly variable (12 orders of magnitude!) depending on rock type.

Hydraulic Gradient
    The difference in {term}`hydraulic head` per unit distance, $\dv{h}{l}$.

Hydraulic Head
    The total mechanical energy per unit weight of water at a point, expressed as a height (in m) above a reference datum (usually mean sea level).
    It is the sum of the {term}`elevation head` and the {term}`pressure head`, $h = z + \frac{P}{\rho g}$
    (there is also a kinetic or velocity head, but this is negligible in groundwater systems).
    Groundwater flows from high to low hydraulic head.

Infiltration
    The downward entry of water into soil.

Laminar Flow
    Smooth flow in which the fluid moves in parallel layers with no mixing between them.
    Flow through porous media is laminar at low {term}`Reynolds numbers<Reynolds Number>`, and this is the regime in which {term}`Darcy's law` holds.
    Compare {term}`turbulent flow`.

Ocean Acidification
    A reduction in ocean pH caused by the dissolution of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ into water.

Percolation
    The downward movement of water through soil.

Permeability
    The physical characteristics of a porous medium that allow water to flow through it. Related to {term}`porosity`, but not identical - a porous rock is not necessarily permeable.

Porosity
    The total sum of all void spaces in a rock. Often expressed as a percentage, or a number between 0 and 1. Not all these spaces may be connected, however, so for water flow, the *effective porosity* is the amount of void space that can hold and transmit water.

Potentiometric Surface
Piezometric Surface
    The level to which water would rise in a well that taps an {term}`aquifer`; a surface showing the {term}`hydraulic head` across the aquifer.
    In an {term}`unconfined aquifer` it coincides with the {term}`water table`.

Pressure Head
    The pressure of the water at a point divided by $\rho g$, expressed in m.
    It is the height of the column of water that the pressure at that point would support.

Pumping Test
    A field test in which water is pumped from a well at a known rate while the {term}`drawdown` is measured in nearby observation wells,
    used to determine the {term}`hydraulic conductivity` and {term}`transmissivity` of an {term}`aquifer`.

Recharge
    The downward movement of water from the soil to the {term}`water table<Water Table>`.

Residence Time
    The average time that a given substance remains in a reservoir.

Reynolds Number
    The ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces within a fluid. Fluids with a large Reynolds Number are dominated by inertial forces, and fluids with a small Reynolds Number are dominated by viscous drag.

Saline Intrusion
    The movement of saline groundwater into a freshwater {term}`aquifer`, usually caused by {term}`abstraction` near the coast or by sea-level rise.

Solubility Pump
    The movement of carbon around the ocean by physical-chemical processes. Surface water cools as it travels to higher latitude, allowing it to store more C and DIC. This downwells into the deep ocean, where it remains until that water is upwelled. The warming of upwelling water releases the stored C back into the atmosphere.

Specific Discharge
Darcy Velocity
    The flow rate through a system divided by its cross sectional area, $q = Q/A$.

Steady State
    A condition in which the amount of something stored in a system does not change with time, because the inputs balance the outputs.

Storativity
    The volume of water released from storage per unit decline in hydraulic head in the aquifer, per unit area of the aquifer.

Total Alkalinity
    A conservative quantity that can be used to track the equilibrium state of weak acids in water. Calculated as the sum of the negative ions of weak acids, minus $\mathrm{[H^+]}$, this does not change as acid-base equilibria shift with temperature because it includes both the acid and base component of the system.

Transmissivity
    The ability of an {term}`aquifer` to transmit groundwater throughout its entire saturated thickness,
    equal to the {term}`hydraulic conductivity` multiplied by the saturated thickness, $T = Kb$.

Turbulent Flow
    Chaotic flow with eddies and mixing, which occurs at high {term}`Reynolds numbers<Reynolds Number>`.
    In porous media, the onset of non-linear and turbulent flow means {term}`Darcy's law` no longer holds.
    Compare {term}`laminar flow`.

Unconfined Aquifer
    An {term}`aquifer` where the {term}`water table<Water Table>` defines the upper water limit.

Water Footprint
    The total amount of water it takes to manufacture something.

Water Table
    The upper surface of groundwater, or the level below which an {term}`aquifer` is permanently saturated with water.

```
